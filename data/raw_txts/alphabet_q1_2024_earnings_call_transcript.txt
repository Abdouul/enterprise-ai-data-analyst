PREPARED REMARKS — SUNDAR PICHAI
================================================================================

"Thank you Jim. Q1 was a strong start to the year, with momentum across Search,
Cloud, and YouTube. Total revenues grew 15% year-over-year to $80.5 billion,
and we saw improving profitability driven by cost discipline and operating
leverage in our largest businesses.

Let me spend a few minutes on AI, which is central to everything we are doing.

Google Search has been transformed by AI. Our introduction of AI Overviews in
Search — formerly called Search Generative Experience — is now available to
hundreds of millions of users globally. Early data shows AI Overviews are
increasing Search engagement, particularly for complex, multi-step queries where
users previously might have given up or turned to multiple searches. Monetization
of AI Overviews is progressing in line with our expectations, with ads appearing
alongside AI-generated summaries at CPMs that are comparable to traditional
search ads.

On the Cloud side, Google Cloud's revenue grew 28% to $9.6 billion. AI is a
meaningful and growing driver — we are seeing strong adoption of Vertex AI,
our managed AI platform, and Gemini for enterprise customers. We now have over
1,000 enterprise customers with AI deals over $1 million in annual contract value.

DeepMind's Gemini 1.5 Pro, with its industry-leading 1 million token context
window, is garnering strong interest from enterprise developers who need to
analyze large codebases, lengthy legal documents, or entire financial reports
in a single API call. Gemini 1.5 Flash, our efficient model, is already serving
millions of API calls daily.

We are also making significant hardware progress. The sixth generation Google
TPU, called Trillium, delivers 4.7x improvement in compute performance per chip
versus TPU v5. This proprietary AI silicon strategy reduces our dependence on
NVIDIA GPU supply and gives us a structural cost advantage in training and
serving large language models at scale."

GOOGLE CLOUD PLATFORM (GCP) — COMPETITIVE POSITIONING
================================================================================

Google Cloud has emerged as a credible number-three cloud provider, with
differentiation centered on:

1. AI/ML INFRASTRUCTURE:
   - TPU v5p pods available to cloud customers, offering competitive price-
     performance for model training vs. NVIDIA GPU clusters
   - Vertex AI: End-to-end managed ML platform with integrations for
     Gemini, Llama, Mistral, and custom open-source models
   - AlloyDB for PostgreSQL with built-in vector search for RAG applications
   - BigQuery ML: SQL-based ML model training directly within the data warehouse

2. DATA ANALYTICS:
   - BigQuery: Google's serverless data warehouse remains a market leader for
     analytical workloads. New BigQuery features include:
     * Continuous queries (streaming SQL on real-time data)
     * BigQuery omni (cross-cloud query across AWS S3 and Azure Blob)
     * Native Gemini integration for natural language-to-SQL

3. WORKSPACE ENTERPRISE AI:
   - Gemini for Workspace (formerly Duet AI): AI assistant embedded in Gmail,
     Docs, Sheets, Meet, and Chat for summarization, drafting, and analysis.
     Approximately 1 million paying Workspace AI customers as of Q1 2024.

CLOUD CHALLENGES:
   - AWS and Azure continue to dominate enterprise market share (AWS ~33%,
     Azure ~23%, GCP ~11% of global cloud spend per Synergy Research)
   - Perception of enterprise account management and support quality trailing
     AWS/Azure, though improving
   - Multi-cloud complexity: Many enterprises maintain primary workloads on
     AWS or Azure and use GCP selectively for BigQuery or AI services

YOUTUBE MONETIZATION AND COMPETITION
================================================================================

YouTube generated $8.1 billion in advertising revenue in Q1 2024, growing 21%
year-over-year, reaccelerating from mid-single-digit growth in 2022-2023.

Growth drivers:
  - Connected TV (CTV): YouTube is now the #1 streaming service on US
    televisions by watch time, enabling premium CPMs from brand advertisers
  - YouTube Shorts: Short-form video ad revenue has crossed profitability
    threshold; Shorts now generates over 70 billion daily views globally
  - Direct Response: YouTube has gained share in performance advertising
    due to improved conversion tracking and AI-powered campaign automation
    (Performance Max campaigns)

YouTube Premium subscribers: ~100 million (ad-free tier + YouTube Music)
YouTube TV (live TV streaming): ~8 million subscribers

Competition:
  TikTok remains the primary competitive threat for short-form video engagement,
  particularly with users under 35. The potential US TikTok ban (if upheld by
  courts) could materially benefit YouTube Shorts by redirecting creator and
  advertiser attention.

WAYMO AUTONOMOUS VEHICLE UPDATE (Other Bets)
================================================================================

Waymo One, Alphabet's robotaxi subsidiary, is operating fully autonomous
commercial service in:
  - San Francisco, CA: ~100 vehicles, available to general public
  - Phoenix, AZ (Chandler/Tempe/Scottsdale): ~300 vehicles
  - Los Angeles, CA: Expanding early access program

Waymo completed over 1 million paid robotaxi trips in 2023, with public waitlists
indicating strong consumer demand. The company plans to expand to Austin, TX
and Atlanta, GA in 2024-2025.

Key economic challenge: Each Waymo vehicle carries approximately $500,000-
$1,000,000 in sensor hardware (LiDAR arrays, cameras, compute) plus ongoing
maintenance and operations costs. At current scale, the robotaxi economics
are deeply unprofitable. Waymo's path to profitability requires:
  - Hardware cost reduction: Next-generation sensor hardware is expected to
    cost 50% less than current generation
  - Fleet scaling: Fixed costs spread across larger fleets improve unit economics
  - Trucking expansion (Waymo Via): Long-haul autonomous trucking could offer
    better unit economics than passenger robotaxi at scale

Waymo raised $5.6 billion in a secondary funding round in October 2023 from
Alphabet and external investors, providing approximately 5-7 years of runway.

RISKS
================================================================================

1. Search disruption from AI: If users shift to AI chatbots (ChatGPT, Copilot)
   for information-seeking tasks previously handled by Search, Google's core
   revenue stream could be materially impacted. Management believes AI Overviews
   strengthens Search's position but acknowledges this is the most important
   strategic question facing the company.

2. EU regulatory risk: The Digital Markets Act designates Google Search, Chrome,
   and Android as gatekeepers, requiring interoperability and non-self-
   preferencing obligations that could impair the integrated Google ecosystem.
   Potential fines of up to 10% of global annual revenue for violations.

3. DOJ antitrust (Search distribution): Trial concluded January 2024. The DOJ
   argued that Google's $15-20 billion annual payments to Apple and others for
   default search placement illegally foreclosed competition. A ruling against
   Google could require termination of these agreements, representing a material
   risk to query volume.

4. AI Overviews monetization uncertainty: Cannibalization of organic search
   clicks by AI-generated summaries could reduce ad inventory over time.

5. CapEx intensity: Sustained $48B+ annual CapEx requires strong revenue growth
   to maintain return on invested capital.
