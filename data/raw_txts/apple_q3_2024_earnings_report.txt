APPLE INC.
QUARTERLY EARNINGS REPORT — Q3 FISCAL YEAR 2024
For the Three Months Ended June 29, 2024

================================================================================
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

Apple Inc. reported third-quarter fiscal 2024 results with total net revenue of
$85.8 billion, representing a 5% increase year-over-year. Diluted earnings per
share reached $1.40, an increase of 11% compared to the same period last year.
The company generated operating cash flow of $29.0 billion during the quarter.

CEO Tim Cook commented: "We are happy to report that our installed base of active
devices reached an all-time high in all geographic segments, and we are excited
about the opportunities ahead across our business."

SUPPLY CHAIN AND OPERATIONAL RISKS
================================================================================

SUPPLY CHAIN CONCENTRATION RISK:
Apple's manufacturing operations remain heavily concentrated in Asia, primarily
in China, Taiwan, and Vietnam. Approximately 90% of iPhone assembly is conducted
by Foxconn Technology Group and Pegatron Corporation in their Chinese facilities.
This concentration exposes the company to geopolitical risk, including potential
trade restrictions, tariffs, and diplomatic tensions between the United States
and the People's Republic of China.

During Q3 2024, Apple continued its multi-year diversification strategy by
expanding production capacity in India (Tata Electronics and Foxconn India) and
Vietnam. The India facilities now account for approximately 14-18% of global
iPhone production, up from less than 1% in 2020.

COMPONENT SUPPLY RISKS:
  - Advanced semiconductor shortages remain a concern. Apple's A17 Pro and M4
    chips are manufactured exclusively by TSMC on its 3nm process node in
    Taiwan. Any disruption to TSMC's Taiwan operations — whether from natural
    disaster, geopolitical conflict, or capacity constraints — could materially
    impact Apple's ability to supply products to market.
  - Rare earth materials used in magnets, displays, and batteries are sourced
    predominantly from China. Export controls imposed by Beijing in late 2023 on
    gallium and germanium have increased input costs and prompted Apple to seek
    alternative sourcing agreements.
  - Memory components (NAND flash and DRAM) are sourced from Samsung, SK Hynix,
    and Micron. Pricing volatility in the memory market impacted gross margins
    by an estimated 40-60 basis points in the quarter.

LOGISTICS AND FREIGHT:
  Disruptions to Red Sea shipping lanes due to Houthi attacks on commercial
  vessels have forced Apple's logistics partners to reroute shipments via the
  Cape of Good Hope, adding approximately 10-14 days to transit times from
  Asian factories to European distribution centers. The company has absorbed
  incremental air freight costs of approximately $180-220 million in the first
  half of fiscal 2024 to mitigate retail stock-out risk.

REGULATORY RISK — EUROPEAN UNION:
  The EU Digital Markets Act (DMA), which came into effect in March 2024,
  requires Apple to allow third-party app stores and alternative payment systems
  on iOS devices sold within the European Union. Apple's compliance approach has
  been challenged by the European Commission and several large developers,
  creating uncertainty around the long-term revenue model for the App Store in
  the region. The EU App Store contributed an estimated $3.1-3.8 billion in
  annual Services revenue, representing approximately 3.5% of total Services.

================================================================================
SERVICES SEGMENT DEEP DIVE
================================================================================

Apple's Services segment achieved another record quarter with $24.2 billion in
revenue, growing 14% year-over-year. The segment now represents 28.2% of total
company revenue, up from 26.4% in the same period last year.

Services gross margin reached 74.0%, significantly above the company-wide
average, reflecting the high-margin, asset-light nature of digital services.

KEY SERVICES COMPONENTS:
  App Store:                    ~$8.5B (est.)
  Apple Music & Podcasts:       ~$2.5B (est.)
  iCloud+ Storage:              ~$3.2B (est.)
  Apple TV+:                    ~$1.9B (est.)
  AppleCare & Licensing:        ~$8.1B (est.)

The company's advertising business within the App Store continued strong growth,
with search ad pricing rising due to increased competition from app developers
seeking premium placement. Apple's advertising revenue is estimated at
approximately $7.1 billion on an annualized basis.

CAPITAL ALLOCATION
================================================================================

Apple returned $32 billion to shareholders during Q3 FY2024:
  Share Repurchases:             $26.0B
  Dividends:                      $3.9B
  Dividend per Share:             $0.25 (increased from $0.24)

The Board of Directors authorized an additional $110 billion share repurchase
program in May 2024, one of the largest buyback authorizations in corporate
history. Total cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities stood at
$153.0 billion at quarter end, against total debt of $101.3 billion.

================================================================================
FORWARD-LOOKING GUIDANCE (Q4 FY2024)
================================================================================

For Q4 Fiscal 2024 (ending September 2024), Apple expects:
  - Total revenue of approximately $85-88 billion
  - Services revenue growth in the low double-digit percentage range YoY
  - Gross margin between 45.5% and 46.5%
  - Operating expenses between $14.2 billion and $14.4 billion
  - Other income/(expense) of approximately $300 million
  - Tax rate of approximately 16.5%

The company noted that it anticipates a partial-quarter contribution from
Apple Intelligence features, its generative AI platform, which began rolling out
to users in beta form during Q4.

RISK FACTORS SUMMARY
================================================================================

1. Geopolitical risk between US and China affecting manufacturing and sales
2. TSMC Taiwan concentration risk for advanced chip supply
3. European regulatory risk under the Digital Markets Act
4. Foreign exchange headwinds (strong USD vs. CNY, JPY, EUR)
5. Consumer discretionary spending sensitivity in a high-interest-rate environment
6. Competitive pressure in China from Huawei and domestic Android OEMs
7. Antitrust investigations in the US, EU, and UK regarding App Store practices
8. Privacy regulation increasing compliance costs globally
