EXXONMOBIL CORPORATION
Q2 2024 EARNINGS REPORT AND OPERATIONAL UPDATE
For the Three Months Ended June 30, 2024

================================================================================
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

ExxonMobil reported second-quarter 2024 earnings of $9.2 billion ($2.14 per
share diluted), supported by strong upstream production performance, partially
offset by weaker refining margins versus the prior year period. The company
completed its transformative $60 billion acquisition of Pioneer Natural Resources
in May 2024, adding the largest acreage position in the Permian Basin and
materially changing the company's long-term production growth profile.

CEO Darren Woods stated: "The Pioneer acquisition positions ExxonMobil as the
largest operator in the most prolific oil field in the world. The Permian
Basin's combination of scale, resource quality, and breakeven economics below
$35/barrel makes it the most compelling energy investment opportunity globally.
We are already capturing synergies exceeding our initial projections."

ENERGY TRANSITION STRATEGY: LOW CARBON SOLUTIONS
================================================================================

ExxonMobil has positioned itself as an "energy solutions" company, arguing that
the world needs affordable, reliable energy alongside decarbonization — and
that ExxonMobil's engineering expertise can address both.

LOW CARBON SOLUTIONS SEGMENT OVERVIEW:
  Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS):
    ExxonMobil is the global leader in CCS by volume, having captured and
    stored approximately 40% of all man-made CO2 ever sequestered commercially.
    The company is pursuing major new CCS projects:

    - Gulf Coast CCS Hub: ExxonMobil is developing an industrial-scale carbon
      capture network in the Houston Ship Channel area, targeting 50 million
      tons of CO2 per year of capture capacity by 2030. This project, if
      successful, would be the largest CCS project in the world. Phase 1
      targets 10 MTPA by 2025.
    
    - Customer CCS agreements: ExxonMobil has signed agreements with 11 industrial
      customers (including LyondellBasell, Linde, Air Products, CF Industries)
      to capture, transport, and store CO2 from their facilities.

  Hydrogen:
    - Hydrogen production from natural gas with CCS (blue hydrogen): Feasibility
      study complete for a 1 GW blue hydrogen facility at Baytown, TX.
    - Target: Produce 1 billion cubic feet/day of hydrogen by 2030

  Lithium:
    - ExxonMobil entered direct lithium extraction (DLE) in 2023, acquiring
      drilling rights to lithium-rich brine formations in the Smackover
      formation in Arkansas.
    - Target: 100,000 tonnes/year lithium production by 2030
    - DLE technology extracts lithium from subsurface brine without the
      large evaporation ponds used in traditional lithium production
    - Strategic rationale: Leverage existing subsurface expertise;
      address projected lithium supply deficits for EV batteries

REFINING AND CHEMICAL OUTLOOK
================================================================================

REFINING MARGINS PRESSURE:
  Global refining margins normalized significantly from the exceptional 2022-2023
  period, when post-COVID demand recovery and Russian crude/product sanctions
  created unusually tight product markets. Current refining margins (Q2 2024):
  - US Gulf Coast Crack Spread: ~$18/bbl (vs. ~$38/bbl peak in 2022)
  - European Crack Spread: ~$12/bbl
  
  Drivers of margin normalization:
  - New refinery capacity additions in Middle East (ADNOC Ruwais, Saudi Aramco)
  - Weaker jet fuel and diesel demand in Europe and China
  - US SPR drawdown cycle complete; ending strategic inventory tailwind

CHEMICAL MARGINS:
  Polyethylene and polypropylene margins remain compressed due to:
  - Overcapacity from major Chinese petrochemical expansions (2022-2024)
  - Weak end-market demand from packaging and durable goods industries
  - US Gulf Coast producers facing margin pressure from Chinese capacity

OIL PRICE SENSITIVITY AND CAPITAL DISCIPLINE
================================================================================

ExxonMobil's capital program is structured to be economically resilient across
oil price scenarios:

  Scenario: Oil @ $60/bbl Brent
    Free Cash Flow (after $28B capex):    ~$5B (sufficient to cover dividend)
    Dividend coverage: Maintained

  Scenario: Oil @ $80/bbl Brent (management base case)
    Free Cash Flow:                       ~$14B
    Shareholder returns:                  ~$8-10B/year (buybacks + dividends)

  Scenario: Oil @ $100/bbl Brent
    Free Cash Flow:                       ~$25B+
    Accelerated buyback opportunity

FINANCIAL FRAMEWORK PRIORITIES (in order):
  1. Maintain and grow dividend (raised annually for 41 consecutive years)
  2. Fund capital program through the cycle
  3. Maintain strong balance sheet (target leverage ratio: ~10-15% debt/capital)
  4. Return surplus cash through buybacks ($17.5B authorized for FY2024)

================================================================================
RISKS
================================================================================

1. Oil price volatility: ExxonMobil's earnings are highly sensitive to
   commodity prices. A $10/bbl change in Brent crude impacts annual earnings
   by approximately $2.5 billion at current production levels.

2. Energy transition acceleration risk: Faster-than-expected EV adoption and
   renewable energy deployment could reduce long-term hydrocarbon demand,
   affecting the economics of Permian Basin and other long-lived assets.

3. Pioneer integration execution: Integrating the largest acquisition in
   ExxonMobil's history while maintaining operational performance creates
   management bandwidth and cultural integration risks.

4. Regulatory and legal: ExxonMobil is engaged in litigation with activist
   investor Follow This and ESG-focused shareholder groups that have proposed
   more aggressive emissions reduction targets. The company's pre-emptive
   lawsuit against the investors was dismissed by US courts, creating
   reputational risk.

5. Natural gas price: Henry Hub below $2/MMBtu is uneconomical for most
   US gas-directed drilling and depresses LNG project economics.

6. Geopolitical: Operations in Iraq (West Qurna), Kazakhstan (Kashagan),
   and other jurisdictions subject to sovereign risk, resource nationalism,
   and contract renegotiation.
