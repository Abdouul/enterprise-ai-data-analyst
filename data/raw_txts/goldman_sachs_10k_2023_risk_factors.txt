ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS
================================================================================

The following is a summary of the principal risks that could materially and
adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations.
This summary should be read in conjunction with the full risk factor disclosures
in Goldman Sachs' complete Form 10-K filing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MARKET AND LIQUIDITY RISKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MARKET RISK — INTEREST RATE SENSITIVITY
Our businesses are materially affected by conditions in the global financial
markets, particularly interest rate levels and volatility. In 2023, we
experienced the impact of the most aggressive Federal Reserve rate-hiking cycle
since the 1980s, with the federal funds rate rising to a 22-year high of
5.25-5.50%. This environment has had mixed effects:

  Positive impacts:
    - Net interest income on our banking and lending portfolio has expanded
      as floating-rate assets reprice upward
    - Asset and Wealth Management fee income benefits from higher cash sweep
      rates and money market fund yields
    - Fixed income intermediation revenues benefit from elevated volatility

  Negative impacts:
    - Investment banking deal volumes (M&A, IPO) remain significantly below
      historical averages due to buyer-seller valuation gaps caused by higher
      discount rates
    - Commercial real estate (CRE) portfolio faces increased stress as
      property valuations have declined 20-30% from peak in certain segments
    - Loan loss provisions have increased for consumer lending (Marcus platform)

CREDIT RISK — COMMERCIAL REAL ESTATE
Our exposure to commercial real estate credit risk has become a heightened
focus of regulators and investors. Key exposures include:

  - Office CRE: Our outstanding office CRE loans and securities total
    approximately $4.9 billion. Office vacancy rates in major US markets
    (San Francisco: 32%, New York: 19%, Chicago: 22%) have continued to
    rise following COVID-era shift to hybrid work. Many borrowers face
    refinancing risk as loans originated in 2019-2021 at low rates mature
    and must be refinanced at rates 300-400bps higher.

  - Multifamily Housing: Rising insurance costs and property taxes, combined
    with rent control ordinances in certain markets, are compressing net
    operating income for multifamily CRE borrowers.

  We have established specific reserves of $1.2 billion against our CRE
  portfolio, representing approximately 3.8% of total CRE exposure.

LIQUIDITY RISK
As a global systemically important bank (G-SIB), Goldman Sachs is subject to
enhanced liquidity requirements under Basel III/IV and the Federal Reserve's
Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) and Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) rules.

  Our Liquidity Coverage Ratio as of December 31, 2023: 127%
  (Minimum regulatory requirement: 100%)

  Global Core Liquid Assets (GCLA): $422 billion
  
  GCLA composition:
    U.S. Treasury Securities:          $189B (44.8%)
    U.S. Agency Securities:             $87B (20.6%)
    Non-US Sovereign Securities:        $72B (17.1%)
    Cash at Central Banks:              $74B (17.5%)

The March 2023 failures of Silicon Valley Bank and Signature Bank demonstrated
the speed at which digital bank runs can materialize in an environment of social
media-amplified depositor anxiety. While Goldman Sachs' funding base is
institutionally diversified and thus less susceptible to retail deposit flight,
we continue to monitor wholesale funding markets for signs of stress.

OPERATIONAL AND TECHNOLOGY RISKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CYBERSECURITY RISK
Financial institutions remain primary targets for sophisticated cyber attacks
from nation-state actors, criminal organizations, and hacktivists. Our
Cybersecurity Risk Framework governs threat detection, incident response,
and business continuity.

Significant cybersecurity developments in 2023 affecting the financial sector:
  - MOVEit file transfer vulnerability (CVE-2023-34362): The Cl0p ransomware
    group exploited this zero-day to steal data from hundreds of organizations
    globally. Goldman Sachs confirmed that data from third-party vendor
    PBI Research Services, used for pension benefit administration, was
    potentially affected, impacting data associated with approximately
    80,000 current and former employees and customers.
  - Increased attacks on SWIFT interbank messaging infrastructure targeting
    correspondent banking relationships
  - AI-generated social engineering (deepfake voice fraud, AI-generated phishing)
    represents an emerging threat vector requiring enhanced authentication

We have invested $1.3 billion annually in cybersecurity, employing approximately
1,900 dedicated cybersecurity professionals.

CONDUCT AND LEGAL RISK
The firm continues to face legal and regulatory proceedings in multiple
jurisdictions:

  1MDB Settlement Legacy: The $2.9 billion global settlement related to the
  1Malaysia Development Berhad fraud (reached in 2020) continues to impose
  compliance obligations, including ongoing monitoring by the US Department of
  Justice, Federal Reserve, and regulators in Malaysia, Singapore, and the UK.
  Our ability to pursue certain business activities in affected markets remains
  restricted.

  CFPB Inquiry (Marcus Consumer Lending): Following announced wind-down of
  Marcus consumer personal loans and Apple Card partnership restructuring,
  the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau initiated inquiry regarding loan
  servicing practices and treatment of borrowers. We have cooperated fully
  with this inquiry and do not expect material financial liability, but the
  regulatory relationship warrants ongoing monitoring.

  ESG Greenwashing Claims: The SEC's Division of Examinations has conducted
  multiple inquiries into ESG disclosures across the asset management industry.
  Goldman Sachs Asset Management reached a $4 million settlement with the SEC
  in October 2022 related to ESG policies and procedures. We continue to
  enhance our ESG data governance and disclosure practices.

STRATEGIC AND BUSINESS RISKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CONSUMER BANKING STRATEGY EXIT
In October 2023, Goldman Sachs announced the wind-down of its Marcus consumer
banking platform, reversing a strategic initiative launched in 2016 that had
consumed approximately $3-4 billion in cumulative investment and losses.

The consumer banking venture failed to achieve scale for several reasons:
  - Customer acquisition costs were high ($350-500 per account) relative to
    the thin margins available in consumer banking
  - Goldman's brand resonated poorly with mass-market consumers seeking a
    full-service banking relationship
  - Regulatory capital requirements for consumer lending are burdensome for
    a firm historically optimized for institutional business
  - The Apple Card partnership, while generating attractive card volume,
    required Goldman to absorb disproportionate credit risk and servicing costs

Goldman has agreed in principle to transfer the Apple Card partnership to a new
issuer. The total pre-tax write-downs associated with the consumer strategy
wind-down total approximately $3.0-3.5 billion over 2022-2023.

INVESTMENT BANKING CYCLE DEPENDENCY
Goldman Sachs' earnings are highly cyclical, with Investment Banking revenues
representing approximately 15-20% of total revenue in normal environments.
FY2023 Investment Banking revenue was $6.0 billion, down 59% from the FY2021
peak of $14.7 billion. The recovery trajectory is dependent on:
  - M&A activity returning as buyer-seller valuation gaps close
  - IPO market reopening (2023 was the second-weakest IPO market since 2003)
  - Private equity-backed exit transactions resuming as PE firms face LP
    pressure to return capital

ASSET & WEALTH MANAGEMENT PERFORMANCE
Management targets $10 billion in annual fee-based Asset & Wealth Management
revenues by 2025. Progress in 2023 was solid, with fee-based revenues of $7.8
billion, but achieving the target requires:
  - Sustained inflows into alternative investment strategies (private equity,
    private credit, infrastructure, real estate)
  - Harvesting unrealized gains in the on-balance-sheet investing portfolio
    (which contributed to earnings volatility)
  - Expanding the ultra-high-net-worth client base, particularly in Asia Pacific

REGULATORY AND CAPITAL RISKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

BASEL III ENDGAME
The Federal Reserve's Basel III Endgame proposal (July 2023), if adopted as
proposed, would require the largest US banks to hold approximately 19% more
capital. Goldman Sachs would be among the most affected banks given its
trading-intensive business model. We estimate that the full proposal would
require an increase in our Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) ratio target by
approximately 3-4 percentage points.

Current CET1 Ratio: 14.5% (as of December 31, 2023)
Minimum Regulatory Requirement: 13.0% (including G-SIB surcharge)

A final rule is expected in 2024-2025, with a multi-year implementation period.
We anticipate the final rule will be revised to be less punitive than the
initial proposal following extensive industry comment and Congressional scrutiny.
