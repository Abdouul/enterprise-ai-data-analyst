"This was another exceptional quarter. Demand for NVIDIA's AI infrastructure
has broadened from large cloud service providers to enterprises, national AI
initiatives, and sovereign governments. We are no longer selling chips — we
are selling AI factories.

The Hopper GPU architecture (H100, H200) remains capacity-constrained, and we
are working closely with our supply partners TSMC, CoWoS advanced packaging
suppliers, and memory manufacturers to expand supply as rapidly as possible.
Blackwell, our next-generation architecture, is on track for production ramp
beginning in Q3 FY2025 and will represent the largest new product cycle in our
company's history.

The transition from general-purpose computing to accelerated computing is
fundamentally reshaping data center economics. Every dollar spent on NVIDIA GPUs
generates returns in the form of reduced inference costs, faster model training,
and new revenue opportunities from AI-powered products. We estimate that the
world's installed base of data center compute will be entirely replaced over
the next four to five years with accelerated computing infrastructure."

DATA CENTER SEGMENT: DEEP ANALYSIS
================================================================================

The Data Center segment — comprising compute GPUs, networking (InfiniBand,
Ethernet), and DGX systems — is now the overwhelming driver of NVIDIA's business,
representing 87% of total revenue.

H100 / H200 GPU DEMAND DRIVERS:
  1. Foundation model training: Training frontier models (GPT-4 class and above)
     requires clusters of 10,000-100,000 H100s running for weeks to months.
     The top 5 foundation model companies (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind,
     Meta AI, xAI) each operate or are building GPU clusters of 50,000+ units.

  2. Inference infrastructure: Serving AI models at scale requires massive
     inference compute. ChatGPT alone is estimated to process over 10 million
     queries per day, each requiring substantial GPU cycles.

  3. Sovereign AI: National governments are building domestically controlled
     AI infrastructure. Countries that have announced national AI computing
     initiatives include France (Mistral/GENCI), UAE (G42), Saudi Arabia
     (SDAIA), India (IndiaAI), Japan (AIST), and over 20 others. These
     sovereign customers are less price-sensitive than commercial hyperscalers.

  4. Enterprise AI: Fortune 500 companies deploying RAG systems, document
     intelligence, AI-powered customer service, and code generation are
     beginning to acquire direct GPU infrastructure rather than relying
     exclusively on cloud rental.

NVIDIA NETWORKING (FORMERLY MELLANOX):
  InfiniBand high-speed networking is a critical complement to GPU compute.
  GPU clusters of 10,000+ units require ultra-low-latency, high-bandwidth
  interconnects to distribute model weights and synchronize gradient updates
  across thousands of parallel training processes. NVIDIA's InfiniBand holds
  an estimated 80% share of high-performance AI interconnect.

  Ethernet for AI (Spectrum-X): NVIDIA is adapting standard Ethernet for AI
  workloads as an alternative to InfiniBand for inference-heavy deployments,
  expanding the addressable market to organizations unwilling to adopt
  proprietary InfiniBand ecosystems.

BLACKWELL ARCHITECTURE (B100/B200, GB200):
  Blackwell is NVIDIA's next-generation GPU platform, manufactured on TSMC's
  4NP process with custom packaging involving two dies connected via a 10TB/s
  NVLink chip-to-chip interconnect.

  Performance improvements vs. H100:
    - Training throughput: 2.5x improvement
    - Inference throughput: 5x improvement (critical for cost reduction)
    - Memory bandwidth: 8TB/s HBM3e (vs. 3.35TB/s on H100)
    - Power consumption per GPU: ~1000W TDP (requiring new rack power designs)

  The GB200 NVL72 system (a rack-scale supercomputer) combines 36 Grace CPUs
  with 72 Blackwell GPUs, interconnected via NVLink Switch, consuming up to
  120kW per rack. This represents a 10x increase in rack power density versus
  traditional servers, creating significant challenges for data center operators
  regarding power delivery and cooling.

================================================================================
SUPPLY CHAIN CONSTRAINTS
================================================================================

TSMC COWOS PACKAGING:
  H100 and H200 GPUs require CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate) advanced
  packaging, which integrates the GPU die with HBM memory stacks. TSMC's CoWoS
  capacity has been the primary bottleneck in NVIDIA's ability to supply demand.
  TSMC is investing heavily to double CoWoS capacity by end of 2024, but
  allocation remains highly constrained and is rationed among customers including
  AMD, Broadcom, and NVIDIA.

HBM MEMORY:
  High Bandwidth Memory (HBM3/HBM3e) is supplied by SK Hynix, Samsung, and
  Micron. SK Hynix supplies the majority of HBM for H100/H200 GPUs. HBM supply
  is expected to remain tight through 2025 due to the specialized process
  required to manufacture and stack memory dies.

SUPPLY CONCENTRATION RISK:
  Approximately 90% of NVIDIA's advanced GPU manufacturing is concentrated at
  TSMC's Taiwan fabs. Geopolitical risk related to Taiwan remains the most
  significant single supply chain vulnerability. NVIDIA has begun qualifying
  Samsung as an alternative foundry for less critical components, but advanced
  GPU dies are unlikely to be dual-sourced in the near term.

================================================================================
EXPORT CONTROLS AND GEOPOLITICAL RISK
================================================================================

The US Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) has imposed successive rounds of
export controls restricting the sale of advanced AI chips to China:

  October 2022: A100 (equivalent) and H100 restricted; A800/H800 created
  October 2023: A800/H800 also restricted; L20/L40S variants created
  Ongoing: BIS developing performance-based thresholds to close loopholes

China represented approximately 17% of NVIDIA's data center revenue in FY2023.
By Q1 FY2025, this share had declined to an estimated 5-10%, replaced by
demand from US hyperscalers, Middle East sovereign AI, and European enterprises.
The company is developing an H20 chip specifically designed to comply with
current export control thresholds, but its performance is significantly below
H100, limiting adoption for frontier model training.
