PFIZER INC.
FULL YEAR 2023 EARNINGS RESULTS AND PIPELINE UPDATE
For the Year Ended December 31, 2023

================================================================================
OVERVIEW
================================================================================

Pfizer reported full-year 2023 revenues of $58.5 billion, a decline of 42%
from the 2022 record of $100.3 billion. The sharp decline reflects the
normalization of COVID-19 product revenue (Comirnaty vaccine and Paxlovid
antiviral), which had generated $56.7 billion in combined revenue in 2022 but
fell to $11.2 billion in 2023 as pandemic-related demand substantially subsided.

Excluding COVID products, Pfizer's operational revenue grew approximately 7%
year-over-year, demonstrating the underlying strength of its diversified
pharmaceutical portfolio.

The company also completed its $43 billion acquisition of Seagen Inc. in
December 2023, adding a leading antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) platform and
four oncology products with combined annual revenues of approximately $2.2
billion, creating a major new growth engine.

SEAGEN ACQUISITION RATIONALE AND ADC STRATEGY
================================================================================

The $43 billion acquisition of Seagen represents the largest transaction in
Pfizer's history and fundamentally reshapes its oncology pipeline.

ANTIBODY-DRUG CONJUGATES (ADCs):
ADCs are a class of cancer therapeutics that combine a targeted antibody
(which binds to a specific protein on cancer cells) with a highly potent
cytotoxic drug payload, connected by a chemical linker. This "guided missile"
approach concentrates chemotherapy at the tumor site while sparing normal
tissue, improving efficacy and tolerability compared to traditional chemotherapy.

Seagen's ADC legacy:
  - Seagen invented the modern ADC field, developing the first commercially
    approved ADC (Adcetris, 2011)
  - Proprietary linker-payload technology licensed to numerous pharma companies
  - Deep expertise in ADC manufacturing, which requires specialized
    bioconjugation facilities

Seagen Oncology Products (Full-Year Pro Forma Revenue Potential ~$2.5B+):
  1. Padcev (enfortumab vedotin): Urothelial/bladder cancer ADC. FDA-approved
     combination with Keytruda (pembrolizumab) demonstrated transformative
     overall survival benefit in Phase 3 KEYNOTE-A39 trial. Estimated
     peak annual revenue potential: $4-6 billion.
  2. Adcetris (brentuximab vedotin): Hodgkin lymphoma and T-cell lymphoma ADC.
     Mature, but defensible franchise with ~$700M annual revenue.
  3. Tukysa (tucatinib): HER2+ breast/colorectal cancer tyrosine kinase
     inhibitor (not an ADC). Strategic fit with Pfizer's breast cancer franchise.
  4. Tivdak (tisotumab vedotin): Cervical cancer ADC. Smaller opportunity
     but validates ADC manufacturing capabilities.

R&D PIPELINE UPDATE
================================================================================

Pfizer operates one of the pharmaceutical industry's most extensive R&D
pipelines, with 113 programs in clinical development across six therapeutic areas.

ONCOLOGY PIPELINE HIGHLIGHTS:
  - Sigvotatug vedotin (ADC): Phase 3 in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)
    in combination with pembrolizumab. NSCLC represents the highest-value
    oncology opportunity globally. Interim data expected H2 2025.
  - PF-07220060 (CDK4 inhibitor): Phase 3 as endocrine-based therapy for
    HR+/HER2- breast cancer. Differentiated once-daily dosing vs. Ibrance.
  - Elranatamab (BCMAxtCD3 bispecific): Phase 3 in multiple myeloma. Strong
    Phase 2 response rates; competing with J&J's Teclistamab.

VACCINES PIPELINE:
  - RSV vaccine (Abrysvo): Approved for adults 60+ in June 2023. First-mover
    advantage in the RSV adult vaccine market, competing with GSK's Arexvy.
    Estimated peak revenue potential: $2-3 billion annually.
  - mRNA flu vaccine (Phase 3): Leveraging Comirnaty mRNA manufacturing
    platform. Potential to replace traditional egg-based flu vaccine production.
  - Lyme disease vaccine (VLA15): Phase 3, partnership with Valneva. First
    potential Lyme vaccine in over 20 years.

COST REDUCTION PROGRAM
================================================================================

In May 2023, Pfizer announced a multi-year cost realignment program targeting
$3.5 billion in annual net savings by end of 2024. An additional $1.5 billion
in cost savings was announced in October 2023, bringing the total target to
$5.5 billion annually by end of 2025.

Savings categories:
  - Manufacturing network rationalization (closing/consolidating 8 sites)
  - Headcount reduction (~3,200 positions globally)
  - Portfolio prioritization (terminating lower-probability pipeline programs)
  - Procurement consolidation (third-party manufacturing, raw materials)
  - Real estate footprint reduction

The program is necessary to restore margins following the COVID revenue cliff,
maintaining the financial capacity to invest in the Seagen oncology strategy
and late-stage pipeline.

================================================================================
RISK FACTORS SPECIFIC TO 2024
================================================================================

1. COVID demand uncertainty: Government procurement decisions for Paxlovid and
   Comirnaty can shift revenues materially quarter-to-quarter
2. Patent cliffs: Eliquis US patent expires in 2026; preparation for generic
   competition is a strategic priority
3. Seagen integration complexity: Integrating a $43B acquisition while
   simultaneously executing cost restructuring is operationally challenging
4. Drug pricing legislation: The Inflation Reduction Act's Medicare Drug Price
   Negotiation program could significantly reduce revenue from selected products
   (Eliquis is an early candidate for negotiation)
5. ADC manufacturing scale-up: Padcev commercial manufacturing requires
   expansion of specialized bioconjugation capacity — technical risk if ramp
   lags commercial demand
